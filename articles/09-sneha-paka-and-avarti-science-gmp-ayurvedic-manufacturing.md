## 1. The Pharmaceutical Physics of Classical *Sneha Kalpana*
For practicing Ayurveda physicians, clinical outcomes depend directly on whether a prescribed *Tailam* (sesame-oil base) or *Keram* (coconut-oil base) has undergone authentic multi-day ***Sneha Paka*** rather than rushed high-heat boiling or synthetic extract blending.

In classical *Sneha Kalpana*, three components—**Kalka** (1 part fine wet herbal bolus), **Sneha** (4 parts base lipid), and **Drava Dravya** (16 parts aqueous decoction, fresh leaf *Swarasa*, milk, or *Takra*)—are simmered over controlled heat across multiple days (*Kala Prakarsha*) so both water-soluble and lipid-soluble phytochemicals transfer into the lipid matrix before residual moisture evaporates.

## 2. The Three Classical *Sneha Paka* Endpoints & Their Clinical Routes
At **Pulamanthole Mooss Pharmaceuticals (GMP Cert No. 575)**, every batch is tested against classical *Sneha Siddhi Lakshanas* (rolling the *Kalka* into a wick that burns without crackling noise, *Shabdaheena Agni Nikshipta*, and characteristic foam appearance):
1. ***Mridu Paka* (Soft Stage):** *Kalka* retains a trace of moisture and softness; traditionally specified for **Nasya Karma** (trans-nasal instillation).
2. ***Madhyama Paka* (Medium Stage):** *Kalka* is non-sticky, soft, and completely free of moisture; specified for **internal oral administration (*Pana*) and *Anuvasana / Matra Vasti*** (such as [Dhanwantharam Avarti 101](../medicines/12-dhanwantharam-avarti-101.md)).
3. ***Khara Paka* (Firm Stage):** *Kalka* becomes slightly firm without charring (*Dagdhapaka*); optimal for **external *Abhyanga*, scalp application, and *Pizhichil***—ensuring long shelf stability and deep transdermal penetration across our classical oils:
