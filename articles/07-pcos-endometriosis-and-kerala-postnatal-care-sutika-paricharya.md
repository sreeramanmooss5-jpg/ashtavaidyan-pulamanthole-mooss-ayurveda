## 1. Why Menstrual & Ovulatory Regularity Depends on *Agni* and *Apana Vata*
In classical Ayurvedic gynecology (***Sthree Roga***), healthy menstrual rhythm and ovulatory function (*Artava Pravrutti*) depend on two physiological pillars:
1. **Unobstructed Nutrient Transformation (*Rasa Dhatwagni*):** In conditions such as Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (**PCOS**), sluggish metabolic fire (*Mandagni*) and insulin resistance produce excess *Kleda* and *Ama*, obstructing *Artavavaha Srotas* and delaying ovulation.
2. **Downward Pelvic Flow of *Apana Vata*:** Chronic stress, irregular sleep, and suppressing natural urges disturb *Apana Vata*, contributing to spasmodic dysmenorrhea (*Kashtartava*), pelvic endometriosis pain, and irregular cycles.

## 2. The Science of Classical Kerala Postnatal Care (*Sutika Paricharya*)
Following childbirth, the sudden emptying of the gravid uterus leaves a dramatic anatomical and physiological space (*Shoonya Shareera*) where ***Vata Dosha* spikes acutely**. If postpartum *Vata* is not systematically pacified during the first **42 to 90 days (*Sutika Kala*)**, women frequently develop chronic lower back pain, pelvic floor weakness, postpartum blues, and joint stiffness years later.

### Core Elements of Pulamanthole Mooss *Sutika Paricharya*
1. **External *Abhyanga* with [Dhanwantharam Thailam](../medicines/11-dhanwantharam-thailam.md):** Daily warm medicated sesame oil application followed by herbal decoction bathing (*Vethukuli*) to pacify *Vata* and tone paraspinal and pelvic ligaments.
2. **Abdominal Binding (*Udara Veshtana*):** Supporting uterine involution and preventing *Vata* accumulation in the abdominal cavity.
3. **Staged Internal Restoration:** Igniting maternal *Jatharagni* (*Panchakola* / *Pippalyadi* priming), followed by classical [Dasamoolarishtam](../medicines/04-dasamoolarishtam.md) and *Jeerakarishtam* to restore stamina, digestive comfort, and healthy lactation (*Stanya Janana*).

## 3. Frequently Asked Questions (AEO Block)
**Q1: When can classical Ayurvedic postnatal care (Sutika Paricharya) begin after normal delivery vs. C-section?**  
After an uncomplicated normal vaginal delivery, gentle internal digestive priming and external limb oleation typically begin within the first week; after a Cesarean section (LSCS), full abdominal *Abhyanga* and binding are deferred until the surgical incision has completely healed (typically 14–21 days post-delivery), as verified by your obstetrician and Dr. Rosni K.
