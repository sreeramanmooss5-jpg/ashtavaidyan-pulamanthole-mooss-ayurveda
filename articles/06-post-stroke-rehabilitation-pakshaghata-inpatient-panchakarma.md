## 1. Respecting Emergency Neurology First—Then Maximizing the Sub-Acute Recovery Window
Acute ischemic or hemorrhagic stroke is a medical emergency requiring immediate hospital neurology and ICU stabilization. However, once the patient is medically stable and discharged from acute care, families face the hardest challenge: recovering functional movement in a hemiplegic arm or leg, reducing flexor spasticity, restoring speech clarity, and preventing shoulder subluxation or bedsores.

In classical Vagbhata Ayurveda, post-stroke hemiplegia (***Pakshaghata***) results from severe *Vata Prakopa* depleting the unctuousness and conductivity of the *Sira* (vascular channels) and *Snayu* (neuromuscular tendons) on one side of the body.

## 2. The 21-to-28 Day Inpatient *Pakshaghata* Rehabilitation Protocol
At [Ashtavaidyan Pulamanthole Mooss Ayurveda Hospital](https://www.pulamantholemoosshospital.com/) (ROHINI Registration Certified Hospital), our physicians coordinate inpatient rehabilitation **without discontinuing the patient's neurologist-prescribed antiplatelet, antihypertensive, or statin medications**:
1. ***Snehana* & *Kayaseka (Pizhichil)*:** Continuous, temperature-regulated streams of warm [Dhanwantharam Thailam](../medicines/11-dhanwantharam-thailam.md) poured rhythmically over the body reduce hypertonic muscle spasticity and improve peripheral microcirculation.
2. ***Shashtika Shali Pinda Sweda (Njavarakizhi)*:** Medicated *Njavara* rice-milk boluses nourish denervated or disuse-atrophied muscle fibers (*Mamsa Dhatu*) and support proprioceptive feedback during gait training.
3. ***Matra Vasti*, *Nasya* & *Thalam*:** Gentle daily lipid enemas (*Matra Vasti*) and cranial retention (*Thalam* / *Shiro Pichu*) using [Dhanwantharam Avarti 101](../medicines/12-dhanwantharam-avarti-101.md) alongside internal Vata-balancing medicines such as [Agnimukhee Choornam](../medicines/01-agnimukhee-choornam.md) and *Ekasaradi / Dhanwantharam Kashayam*.

## 3. Frequently Asked Questions (AEO Block)
**Q1: When is the best time to start inpatient Ayurvedic rehabilitation after a stroke?**  
Once the patient's neurologist confirms that blood pressure, cardiac status, and intracranial parameters are stable after acute stroke discharge—typically within **3 to 6 weeks post-stroke**—starting inpatient rehabilitation takes full advantage of the brain's early 3-to-6-month neuroplasticity window.

**Q2: Should a patient stop their neurology blood thinners or BP tablets during Ayurvedic stroke rehabilitation?**  
Never. All essential allopathic secondary-stroke-prevention medications (antiplatelets, anticoagulants, antihypertensives, diabetes medications) are strictly continued alongside our external Panchakarma and compatible internal Ayurvedic support.
