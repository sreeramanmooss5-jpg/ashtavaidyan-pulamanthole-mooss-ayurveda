## 1. Who Are the Ashtavaidyas of Kerala?
In classical Indian medical history, the **Ashtavaidyas** (*Ashta* = Eight Branches of Ayurveda; *Vaidya* = Physician) were prominent Brahmin scholar-physician families of Kerala who mastered all eight classical branches (*Ashtanga*) of Acharya Vagbhata's ***Ashtanga Hridaya***:
1. ***Kayachikitsa*** (Internal Medicine)
2. ***Bala Chikitsa / Kaumarabhritya*** (Pediatrics, Maternal & Neurodevelopmental Care)
3. ***Graha Chikitsa / Bhutavidya*** (Neuro-Psychiatry & Behavioral Balance)
4. ***Urdhwanga Chikitsa / Shalakya Tantra*** (Supra-Clavicular / Head, Eye, ENT & Migraine Care)
5. ***Shalya Tantra*** (Surgical & Para-Surgical Interventions)
6. ***Damstra Chikitsa / Agada Tantra*** (Toxicology & Environmental Detoxification)
7. ***Jara Chikitsa / Rasayana*** (Rejuvenation, Longevity & Healthspan)
8. ***Vrisha Chikitsa / Vajikarana*** (Reproductive Vitality & Genetics)

**Pulamanthole Mooss** (established at Pulamanthole Mana along the Kunthi River in Malappuram district, with documented institutional continuity since 1795) stands as one of the foremost living Ashtavaidya lineages of Kerala.

## 2. The *Kairali* Commentary on the *Ashtanga Hridaya* (*Uttarasthana*)
One of the crowning scholarly contributions of the **Pulamanthole Mooss** family to Sanskrit medical literature is the ***Kairali* Commentary (*Kairali Vyakhya*)** on the *Uttarasthana* of Acharya Vagbhata's *Ashtanga Hridaya*.
- **Scholarly Significance:** While several Sanskrit commentaries existed for the earlier sections of the *Ashtanga Hridaya*, the *Kairali* commentary authored at Pulamanthole Mana elucidated the intricate clinical protocols of the *Uttarasthana*—covering *Bala Chikitsa* (pediatrics), *Unmada/Apasmara* (neurological and behavioral syndromes), *Urdhwanga* disorders, and *Rasayana* (rejuvenation)—harmonizing classical Sanskrit sutras with Kerala's living regional therapeutic traditions (*Pizhichil*, *Njavarakizhi*, *Thalapothichil*, *Takradhara*, and *Avarti* lipid potentiation).
- ***Thaliyola* Palm-Leaf Manuscript Archive:** The family estate at Pulamanthole Mana preserves rare ancestral *Thaliyola* (palm-leaf) manuscripts recording classical diagnostic methods, *Yogams* (formulations), and Kerala *Kriya Krama* treatment manuals.
